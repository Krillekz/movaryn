Sneak peeks

Previews of what is being built next. These are earlier than release notes: scope, wording and timing can change. The roadmap tracks the same work at a higher level.

Sneak peek 01

Movaryn: Phase 2

In design · No release date

Phase 2 goes after the two things that slow a kit down most: the model not knowing who you are talking about, and the wait before a usable answer.

The big change: it finds out who the character is

Mentioning an obscure anime or manga character should not make a model pattern-match a name and invent abilities. Phase 2 puts an explicit research step before the first draft:

  • An optional lookup checks the character's series, powers, weapons and silhouette, and shows where each fact came from.
  • If the character cannot be verified, Movaryn says so and asks for the series or a short description instead of guessing.
  • Follow canon and Original kit are separate paths: one respects known abilities, the other uses the design as a starting point and labels what was invented.
  • Research is opt-in and server-side: no silent web requests, no key in the browser, and the app still works with no lookup configured.

Also in this phase

  • Shorter setup before the kit, with no restating the brief back at you.
  • The first move streams before the rest of the answer is finished, so something usable is on screen sooner.
  • A Quick kit path drafts one coherent pass, then offers depth on request instead of opening with an essay.
  • Clear progress states while a lookup runs, so waiting has a reason and an end.
  • A character brief saved with the project, so later chats keep the same understanding.

What it will not do

  • Invent canon for a character it cannot verify.
  • Scrape or upload anything silently.
  • Promise a date before the work is real.

Sneak peek 02

Movaryn: Phase 3

In design · No release date

Phase 3 changes what a kit is for: instead of only describing a fight, the kit can be tried before it is built.

The big change: a sandbox that runs the kit

A local simulation plays the saved moves against simple archetypes and reports what the text actually implies:

  • Startup, active and recovery windows are estimated from the move sequence and drawn as a timeline.
  • Common answers are tested — sidestep, block, counter, ranged poke — and summarised in a short counterplay report.
  • A kit can be tuned in the sandbox before it is exported, using the same limits the selected game enforces.

Also in this phase

  • Frame-data table export alongside Markdown, CSV and JSON.
  • Matchup notes attached to a kit and carried into later drafts.
  • A read-only kit link that renders the moves and the counterplay report.
  • Sandbox presets per game, so JJS and TSB timings do not share one assumption.

What it will not do

  • Claim to be the real game's netcode or physics.
  • Present estimated windows as measured frame data.
  • Upload a kit anywhere without an explicit action.

Sneak peek 03

Movaryn: Phase 4

In design · No release date

Phase 4 treats a good kit as portable: the idea should survive a change of game.

The big change: one kit, remapped to another game

  • A saved kit can be translated into another supported format, with each move rewritten to that game's rules rather than copied across.
  • The remap shows what changed and why: range, cooldown, resource cost and move count.
  • Moves that cannot exist in the target game are flagged with a buildable alternative instead of being dropped quietly.

Also in this phase

  • Rule packs that describe a game's limits once and apply them to every project.
  • Balance scorecards comparing damage, range and commitment across a kit.
  • Import from CSV and clipboard, so old sheets do not have to be retyped.
  • A printable project sheet with the brief, moves, art prompts and kit check.

What it will not do

  • Pretend a remap is a drop-in replacement for the original kit.
  • Invent mechanics for a game it has no rules for.
  • Replace the user's own balance judgement.

Sneak peek 04

Movaryn: Phase 5

In design · No release date

Phase 5 changes where the workshop lives. Local-first stays the default; sync becomes a choice.

The big change: an optional synced workspace

  • Projects can follow you between machines with an account, while local-only projects keep working untouched.
  • Share links open a read-only kit in the browser, with no install and no provider key.
  • Collaborators can comment on moves instead of copying the whole project.

Also in this phase

  • Version history per project, with restore.
  • A browser-first layout that works on a phone for reading and commenting.
  • Team projects with shared house rules.
  • Account-free export stays, so a project can leave at any time.

What it will not do

  • Move existing local projects to a server without asking.
  • Require an account to use the app.
  • Put provider keys anywhere but your own machine.

Sneak peek 05

Movaryn: Character briefs

In design · No release date

A closer look at the brief that Phase 2 introduces. Today each chat starts from whatever the model thinks it remembers, so a name can mean one thing in the first reply and something else after a restart. A character brief makes that understanding explicit, project-scoped and editable.

One brief per character

  • A short brief records the series, which version of the character is meant, the abilities worth keeping and the ones to avoid.
  • Follow canon, Original kit and Mixed are stated modes, not an unspoken assumption. The mode appears on the kit, so a reader can tell what was respected and what was invented.
  • The brief is captured before drafting, and every later reply in the project reuses it — including after a restart or a new chat.
  • Every line the model wrote can be edited or replaced. The brief belongs to the project, and the app never quietly overwrites it.

Where the facts come from

  • Facts are labelled: carried in the brief, taken from a lookup with a source, or invented for an original kit.
  • Switching from Follow canon to Original kit keeps the brief and starts a fresh draft instead of rewriting the earlier one.
  • If the brief and a lookup disagree, Movaryn shows the conflict and asks which one wins rather than picking silently.
  • No brief is required to start. Without one, the app asks the questions it would otherwise guess at.

What this will not do

  • Treat a lookup as canon when it cannot cite a source.
  • Lock a project to one interpretation forever.
  • Hide the canon-or-original choice inside a prompt you cannot see.
